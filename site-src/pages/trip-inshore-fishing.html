The trip most people book

A private boat with Capt. Tanner working the creeks, oyster bars and grass flats from Crystal River to Homosassa for redfish, speckled trout and snook, with cobia, jacks and Spanish mackerel showing up on the outside in the warmer months. Live bait, artificials or both, whatever the fish want that day.

Both lengths fish the same water with the same gear. Four hours covers one stage of the tide and gets you back for lunch; seven hours lets you follow the fish through the swing and run farther when the bite is somewhere else. Tanner picks the start time around the tide and tells you the evening before.

Best for

  • Families and first-timers: Tanner rigs, baits and coaches
  • Kids of any age, with short runs and steady action
  • Anglers who want a redfish, trout and snook slam
  • Visitors with a morning or afternoon to spare

What you’ll catch inshore around Crystal River

Inshore fishing in Crystal River is good twelve months a year. What is biting best changes with the water temperature.

Redfish

Year-round • best September to December

Tailing on the flats in fall, schooled up on the oyster bars in winter. The fish Crystal River is known for.

Speckled trout

Year-round • best November to March

Gator trout over the grass in the cooler months, numbers on the flats the rest of the year.

Snook

March to November

Pushing north every year. Mangrove shorelines and creek mouths on the warmer tides.

Cobia, jacks & mackerel

April to October

The outside bonus on the seven-hour trip when the water warms up. Big pulls on light tackle.

Before you book

Do I need a fishing license?

No. Everyone on board is covered by the boat’s license.

What time do we leave?

Tanner sets the start around the tide, usually between 6:30 and 8 AM for mornings. He texts the time the evening before.

Can we keep the fish?

Yes, within Florida limits and seasons. Your catch is cleaned and bagged on ice at the dock.

What if the weather is bad?

If Tanner calls it, you get a full refund or a new date. Light rain and wind are part of fishing; he decides the morning of.
