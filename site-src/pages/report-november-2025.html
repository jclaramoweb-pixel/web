Crystal River fishing report: November 2025

The shoulder season lives up to the hype: cooling water, clear flats, schooling reds, bigger trout, snook still on the mangroves and shallow gag grouper for anyone willing to run out.

By Capt. Tanner Martin • Posted November 2025 • Crystal River & Homosassa

Hey everyone, Captain Tanner here. November is upon us, and the change of seasons on the Nature Coast is bringing a new rhythm to the water. The heat of summer is gone, the bait is moving, and we’re seeing some of the most interesting shifts of the year in the fishery. Here’s what I’ve been seeing and what you can expect this month.

Conditions and trends

  • Water temperature: The inshore water is cooling into the low to mid 70s during the day, with nights dipping further. That is good news; a lot of predators perk up with that slight drop.
  • Clarity and tide: Flatter weather and calmer wind mean clearer water across the flats and backwaters. Strong tidal movement is the key; the fish feed when the water is moving.
  • Weather: Mornings are crisper and the wind lays down once the peak-season crowds fade. Ideal for being on the water early.

What’s biting in November

Redfish

Schooling reds are still active, and with the clearer water and falling temperatures they are getting more predictable. On the shallow flats you will spot tailing and cruising fish, especially near oyster bars and grass edges. Slow presentations with live bait or cut mullet are working well.

Speckled trout

Trout are stacking up. You might see fewer fish than in the peak of summer, but the average size is going up. The bigger fish are using structure and deeper grass edges as staging areas. Soft plastics, shrimp under popping corks or small live bait fished near structure are producing.

Snook

Snook remain game-on through November. With the cooling water a lot of them move into the deeper backwaters and creek mouths, but they still come out to feed on the flats. Early-morning topwater or jerkbait casts into mangrove roots and sandy potholes are high reward.

Gag grouper and offshore options

If you are willing to run out a bit, the shallow gag grouper bite is rolling strong right now. The water is shallow enough for keeper fish to be reachable. Live pigfish, pinfish or cut bait near reefs and rock in 15 to 30 feet are lighting it up.

Sheepshead and mixed bags

November also brings the sheepshead in around structure: rock piles, docks and hard bottom. They are not the flashiest fight, but they are fun and excellent on the table.

Tips and tactics

  • Timing: Sunrise to mid-morning on a moving tide, and again late afternoon on a decent tide. Fish the full and new moon periods for stronger pushes.
  • Bait and lures: Live mullet, pinfish or cut mullet for reds. Paddle tails or shrimp under a popping cork for trout. Topwater plugs or jerkbaits near the mangroves for snook. Live pigfish or pinfish for shallow gags. Shrimp or fiddler crabs on light jigs for sheepshead.
  • Gear: A medium setup with 20 to 30-pound leader handles most inshore targets. For grouper, move up to 50 to 80-pound and an abrasion-resistant leader.
  • Presentation: Clearer water means stealth wins. Slow your retrieve, keep a low profile and match the forage.

Final thoughts

November is one of those months where the shoulder season lives up to the hype. On the Nature Coast the fishing does not shut down; it evolves. Whether you are chasing cruising reds on the flats, stacking up quality trout, hunting snook or running out for a shot at gag grouper, the opportunities are very much alive.
