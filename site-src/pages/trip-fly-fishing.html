Fly fishing the Crystal River and Homosassa flats

This is the trip for anglers who want to see the fish before they cast. Tanner poles the skinny grass flats of Ozello and Homosassa and the backcountry creeks behind them, one angler on the bow at a time, looking for tailing redfish, cruising snook and trout holding in the potholes. Fly rod or light spinning tackle, your call.

Two anglers is the limit by design: a quiet skiff, room to cast and a captain on the platform reading the water. Bring your own rods or fish Tanner’s. An 8-weight with a floating line and a handful of shrimp and crab patterns covers most days; he has them tied.

Best for

  • Fly anglers who want tailing redfish in clear, shallow water
  • Spin anglers who want to sight-cast with light tackle
  • Anglers learning to fly fish: Tanner teaches casting on the water
  • One or two serious anglers who want the whole boat to themselves

Not the right trip if

  • Your group is three or more (see the Inshore Charter)
  • You want to soak bait and relax (the Inshore Charter does that better)
  • You are after the big tarpon (see Homosassa Tarpon, April to July)

What you’ll cast at

Clear water and shallow grass mean you see most of the fish you catch.

Redfish

Year-round • best September to December

Tailing on the flats on the low incoming. Crab and shrimp patterns, short accurate casts.

Snook

March to November

Laid up along the mangroves and creek mouths. Baitfish patterns, tight to the roots.

Speckled trout

Year-round • best November to March

Big trout in the potholes on the flats. The easiest fish to get a beginner onto with a fly.

Juvenile tarpon

May to September

Backcountry creeks on summer mornings. Ten to forty pounds and a lot of jumping.

Before you book

I have never fly fished. Can I still book this?

Yes. Tanner teaches on the water and keeps light spin tackle rigged as a backup, so nobody goes home without bending a rod.

What rod should I bring?

An 8-weight with a floating line covers redfish, snook and trout. Bring a 10 if you want to chase juvenile tarpon in summer. Or fish Tanner’s.

Why only two anglers?

Sight fishing needs a quiet skiff and room to cast. Three people on an 18-foot boat means somebody is always in the way.

What if it is windy?

Wind is the enemy of sight fishing. Tanner moves to protected creeks and lee shorelines, or calls it and reschedules at no cost.
