Guided fishing in Crystal River and Homosassa

The Nature Coast is the last stretch of Florida’s Gulf side without a beach: spring-fed rivers run through miles of grass flats, oyster bars and mangrove creeks before they reach the Gulf, and that is why the fishing holds up year-round. Redfish and trout live here all twelve months, snook push in from the south, and every spring the Homosassa tarpon migration rolls across the same flats where the fly-rod world records were set.

Capt. Tanner Martin grew up fishing this water and has guided it full time since 2018. He runs three boats so the trip matches the water: a 23-foot Carolina Skiff for inshore groups and scalloping, an 18-foot Hells Bay poling skiff for fly and tarpon, and a Prodigy surface drive for duck hunts and the backwater mud boat trip.

Homosassa Tarpon

Homosassa is where the fly-rod tarpon world records came from, and the migration still rolls across the same clear flats every spring. Eight hours on the water, leaving MacRae’s of Homosassa at 6 AM to be set up on the fish when they start showing.

You will pole and stake out on the travel lanes, watching for rolling fish and daisy chains, then make the cast. It is a patience game with a huge payoff. Bring your 11 or 12-weight or fish Tanner’s; spin tackle is on board for anglers who would rather not fly fish.

From November to February the negative low tides drain the flats and push redfish and trout into the deep holes and channels back in the mangroves, essentially trapping them. More opportunity, less pressure and a better shot at quality fish.

The 1856 Prodigy runs in extremely shallow water and sometimes straight mud to get back where those fish are holding. Four to six hours, up to three anglers, kid and family friendly, with group and corporate trips on as many boats as you need.

Before you book

The questions Tanner gets asked most, answered here so you do not have to call. Anything else, call or text {{PHONE}}.

Do I need a fishing license?

No. Everyone on board is covered by the boat’s license. Duck hunters do need a Florida hunting license with state and federal duck stamps.

Where do we meet?

Pete’s Pier in Crystal River for inshore, fly, scalloping and mud boat trips. MacRae’s of Homosassa for tarpon. Tanner texts the ramp and time the evening before.

How does the deposit work?

$100 holds your date. The balance is due after the trip, by cash or card. Cards carry a 3.5% processing fee, so most people bring cash.

What if I need to cancel?

Cancel more than 14 days out and the deposit comes back. Inside 14 days it is non-refundable, but it is credited to any future trip.

What about weather?

If Tanner calls the trip for weather, you get a full refund or a new date, your choice. If it is fishable, you fish; light rain and wind are part of it.

Can kids come?

Yes. Inshore and scalloping trips are built for families and there is no minimum age. Tarpon and duck hunts are better suited to experienced guests.

Is a tip expected?

A 20% tip for the captain is customary on fishing charters, cash preferred. It is not included in the trip price.

Can we keep the fish?

Yes, within Florida limits and seasons. Tanner cleans your catch at the dock and bags it on ice for the drive home.

Pick your trip and check the calendar

Open dates fill a month or more out in spring and summer. Pick the trip, pick the date and Tanner confirms the same day.
