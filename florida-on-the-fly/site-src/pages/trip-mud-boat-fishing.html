Low tides trap the fish. The mud boat reaches them.

Mud boat fishing is a winter thing in Crystal River and Homosassa. From November through February we get much lower negative tides that drain the flats and push the fish into the deep holes and channels back in the mangroves, essentially trapping them. That means more opportunity, less pressure and a better shot at quality fish.

The boat is an 1856 Prodigy, a mud boat that runs in extremely shallow water and sometimes straight mud, which is how we get back into the areas where the fish are holding. The trips focus on active inshore water where redfish, trout and whatever else is back there are feeding aggressively. It is unlike any other fishing in Crystal River, and it is kid and family friendly.

Best for

  • Winter negative tides, November through February
  • Anglers who want numbers and quality fish with less pressure
  • Families with kids, and group or corporate trips on as many boats as you need

Not the right trip if

  • More than three anglers on one boat (we add a second boat)
  • Anyone who wants shade and a smooth, dry ride
  • Summer dates (the trip runs on winter tides)

The boat

1856 Prodigy mud boat

Up to 3 anglers • Surface drive • Open deck

An 1856 Prodigy: 18 feet long, 56 inches wide, with a surface-drive mud motor that runs over grass, mud and inches of water where an outboard would be stuck. No shade, no cushions, and it gets into creeks the Carolina Skiff and the Hells Bay cannot touch.

Before you book

How many anglers?

Up to three on the Prodigy: $500 for one or two, $100 for the third. Bigger groups and corporate trips go out on multiple boats; call for a quote.

Why the mud boat instead of the skiff?

On the lowest tides the flats drain and the skiffs cannot get back to where the fish go. The surface drive can.

Is it rough?

Rougher than the skiff: mud, grass, no shade and a loud motor. Kids and families do fine on it; the water back there is shallow and calm.

When is it best?

November through February, on the negative low tides. Tanner picks the start time to hit the bottom of the tide.
