Fishing like nowhere else in Florida

Trips start in Kings Bay, where manatees and dolphins are a common sight on the ride out and the sun comes up behind you. From there it's the mangrove creeks, oyster bars and grass flats of Crystal River and Homosassa, chasing the redfish, snook and trout the Nature Coast is known for.

Tanner fishes a corporate group on the Carolina Skiff one day and a single angler on the poling platform with a fly rod the next. Relaxed when it's time to be, dialed in when the fish show up.

"Fish" by hand.

During scallop season, visitors and locals flock to Crystal River for warm, clear water and plentiful bay scallops. With a mask, snorkel and mesh bag, you swim the grass beds in 3 to 6 feet of water and pick them by hand. Easy, safe and one of the most fun days a family can have on the Nature Coast.

We leave MacRae’s at 6 AM, stay on the flat longer than anyone else, and fish hard all day to put a trophy fish in your hands. Spin or fly, up to 2 anglers, strictly catch and release.

Your Day On The Water

Everything to know before you book. Anything else, call Capt. Tanner at (352) 577-4084.

01

Start times

7 AM in summer, 8 AM in fall and winter. Half days wrap up late morning; full days run to 2 PM. Tarpon trips go 6 AM to 2 PM.

02

Where we meet

Pete's Pier in Crystal River for inshore, fly and scallop trips. Tarpon trips leave MacRae's of Homosassa. Duck hunt spots are set before your hunt.

03

What to bring

Lunch, snacks and any drinks besides water (bottled water is on board, alcohol allowed). Sunscreen, a hat and polarized sunglasses.

04

Deposit & weather

$100 deposit to book, balance after the trip. If weather cancels, your deposit moves to a new date, or is refunded if you can’t reschedule.

Your captain

Meet Capt. Tanner Martin, Crystal River fishing guide

“I tell all my clients I try to make them feel like they are fishing with a friend.”

Tanner was born and raised on the Nature Coast. After playing Division 1 baseball at Stetson University and guiding part time through college, he went all in and has run Florida’s On The Fly since 2018. He’ll take a group of four-year-olds catching grunts one day and a fly angler hunting trophy redfish the next, and he offers fly casting lessons for anyone new to the long rod.
